## a)
The origin of paramagnetism and diamagnetism comes from when inspecting a magnetic material on the atomic scale, you would see small currents around each nuclei like small current loops creating magnetic dipoles. These will normally cancel eachother out as they all point in random directions, but when an external B-field is applied, a net alignment of the dipoles occurs turning the material magnetically polarized/magnetized. The direction of magnetization is what determines if its paramagnetic, M is parallell to B, or if its diamagnetic, M is opposite to B.

## b)
Ferromagnetic materials can remain strongly magnetized aftern an external magnetic field because each dipole in the material "likes" to point in the same direction as the other closest dipoles. However this allignement occurs in domains with billions of dipoles that may not allign with another domain. When an external field is applied, the magnetic torque at boundary between two domains will be able to turn some of the dipoles toward allignement of the B-field, turning the material saturated if the field is strong enough. This quality is not usually present in paramagnetic materials, which is why they don't stay magnetized.

## c)
For ferromagnets, the magnet dipole moment is generally more alligned than in para- & diamagnets, leading to a much larger magnitude in the total magnetization thus stronger effects.

## b)
The torque tends to allign the orientation of the magnetic dipole towards what is closest of parallell or opposite to the magnetic field depending on $\theta$. From the right hand rule we can get the direction of how it will rotate, for this case looking at the yz-plane, $m\times B$ leads to "the thumb" going out of the page (x-axis), and the torque will then rotate it along this axis in the direction of the positive z-axis since $\theta$ is < 90 degrees.

## c)
The net forces on the sides of the loop wil cancel when looked at mathematically/in translation, but they will act on different points of the loop leading to the rotational torque. This makes it so that the non-alligned loop will have higher potential energy then the alligned loop, and the torque will use that energy as the work to rotate it back.
