The three fundemental theorems for gradient, divergence and curl are:
$$
 \begin{equation} 
  \text{Gradient: } \int_{a}^{b}(\nabla f)\cdot dl = f(b)-f(a) 
 \end{equation} 
$$
$$
 \begin{equation} 
 \text{Divergence: } \int_{V}^{}(\nabla \cdot A) \ d\tau =  \oint_{S}^{} A \cdot da
 \end{equation} 
$$
$$
 \begin{equation} 
 \text{Curl: } \int_{S}^{} (\nabla \times A) \cdot da = \oint_{L}^{}A \cdot dl
 \end{equation} 
$$
- Gradient theorem gives that with a function $f$, the integral of the gradient of $f$ between two points in any given path is the same as the difference of $f$ given the two points. This gives that the potential of a function between two points can be found with any path.
- Divergence theorem gives that a vector A's flux integral across a surface is the same as the density of flux (divergence) within the surface. This can be seen as the rate of water flowing out of an areas surface is the same as the amount of flow inside the area.
- Curl theorem gives that the flux of the curl through a surface is the same as the line integral across the boundary of the surface. This can be seen as how much swirl a force has in an area is equal to walking around the edge of the area and seeing how much of the force is in line with the edge.
