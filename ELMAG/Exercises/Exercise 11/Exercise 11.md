# 2)
## a)
Magnetic susceptibility is a dimensionless quantity, varying by different materials, that represents the constant of proportionality between the magnetic dipole moment M and the auxiliary field H.
$$
 \begin{equation} 
 M=\chi_{m}H 
 \end{equation} 
$$
For a linear media, we have that:
$$
 \begin{equation} 
 B=\mu_{0}(H+M)=\mu_{0} (1+\chi_{m})H = \mu H
 \end{equation} 
$$
where
$$
 \begin{equation} 
 \mu=\mu_{0}(1+\chi_{m}) 
 \end{equation}. 
$$
## b)
The auxiliary field H can be found using Ampere's law for the free currents running through the solenoid. But the magnetic field B is dependent on the bound currents aswell, and therefore often is calculated later in terms of the magnetic dipole moment and auxiliary fields.

## c)
The magnetic field inside the solenoid changes wether its a paramagnetic or a diamagnetic due to the formula $B=\mu_{0}(1+\chi_{m})H$ where the exponent of $\chi_{m}$ is dependent on the material (positive for paramagnetic, negative for diamagnetic). Thus the magnetic field will be stronger for paramagnetic material and weaker for the diamagnetic. The physical explenation for this is the induced magnetic dipole moment will go with or against the magnetic field depending on the material, either strengthening or weakening the magnetic field.

## d)
I. $\chi_{m}>0$ gives a magnetic dipole moment with the B-field ($+\phi$) and thus $K_{b}$ will point down from the right hand rule of cross product.
II. $\chi_{m}<0$ gives a magnetic dipole moment against the B-field ($+\phi$) and thus $K_{b}$ will point up from the right hand rule of cross product.

## e)
The equation:
$$
 \begin{equation} 
 J_{b}=\nabla \times M=\nabla \times(\chi_{m}H)=\chi_{m}J_{f} 
\end{equation}
$$
This means that the bound currents are proportional to the free currents through the material. This comes from the Ampere's law for the auxiliary field, and the proportionality between B and H. When the free currents run through the material, then the bound currents will only exist on the surface of the material.
